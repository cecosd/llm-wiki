# Learning and writing

## Learn by question

**Learn and write → Learn by question.** You type a question ("how are attachment and anxiety connected?") and get a **reading path** through your own pages: which concepts to start with and in what order.

| Level | What it does |
|---|---|
| Quick | Local, with no AI and no cost. Orders by words and links. |
| Light, Medium, Powerful | The model orders the path and explains why. The more powerful, the more expensive. |

Paths are saved; the "read" marks too. You can limit a path to chosen **areas** and topics.

## Write with the wiki

**Learn and write → Write with the wiki.** You pick a topic and a length, and the program composes an article: abstract, introduction, theoretical framework, analysis, discussion, conclusion and an **APA 7 bibliography**, using only your sources.

- **Locally**: from a template, with no AI.
- **With the AI** on three levels.
- **Continue with the AI**: adds text before "Conclusion" and completes the bibliography. The result is in the editor; "Save" records it, "Undo the addition" removes it.

Drafts are in the `drafts/` folder and can be edited freely; every version is kept in `drafts/.history/` and deleted ones are in `drafts/.trash/`. Citations to printed pages come from the book's `page_offset`.

## Advice for a thesis or an article

The text is a draft for thinking, not finished work. Check every citation in the original (p. N opens the exact page) and rephrase it in your own words.
